01 — The bet

Pick the one thing that has actually worked.

Nothing in the current numbers is working except two Shorts about counterintuitive physics — 1,000 and 750 views with zero promotion. That is the entire signal. Build the whole operation around it.

The site has 720 posts across 43 categories, ~293 of them indexed, an average Google position of 66, and four clicks in the last 28 days. Traffic is trending down. This is what a broad, fast, automated content push looks like after Google's helpful-content and scaled-content updates: the volume works against you — crawl budget spread thin, no topical authority anywhere, no reason for any single page to rank.

Meanwhile the two Shorts that broke out share a shape: one surprising, everyday, physical fact, explained in 60 seconds. That shape is the highest free-distribution format on the internet right now — TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and Google Discover all push it to brand-new accounts without a single backlink or outreach email. It is also the one thing an automated pipeline can produce endlessly without quality collapsing.

The pillar: counterintuitive everyday science

Every day, one fact from the General Science library — the counterintuitive kind. "Heavier objects don't fall faster." "You see lightning before thunder because light basically doesn't travel — sound crawls." "A raise can't shrink your paycheck." One fact becomes six assets (next section). This is the brand: the channel that explains the thing you thought you understood.

The workhorse: Technology Basics, for search only

Keep one slow stream of deep web articles in Technology Basics — "what a VPN actually does," "what the cloud actually is." 100 of these are already written. The intent is evergreen and search-driven, not curiosity-driven, so it doesn't need video. Two genuinely deep pieces a week, heavily interlinked. Nothing else.

Everything else: frozen

The other 41 categories stop receiving new content. They're not deleted — they're parked. Depth in two clusters beats a thin presence in forty-three. You can thaw a category later, from a position of authority, if the data ever asks for it.

03 — Web & search

Fix indexing first. Chase Discover, not rankings.

Get the pages you have into the index

529 URLs sit in "Discovered — currently not indexed" and the count is rising because the firehose is still on. Turning the firehose down is step one. Then: keep the internal-linking pass running (every post linked from ≥3 others), resubmit the sitemap only when something new ships, and spend the daily ~10 URL-inspection indexing requests on the science pillar and the tech workhorse — nothing else.

Prune, hard

720 thin pages is a liability, not an asset. Cut or merge the weakest 30–40% — anything with no impressions after 60 days, near-duplicate coverage, or a locale-specific slug. A smaller site of genuinely useful pages ranks better than a large one Google half-trusts.

Google Discover is the automation-friendly web traffic

Discover doesn't need backlinks or rankings — it needs freshness, a strong lead image, a curiosity headline, and baseline trust signals. Counterintuitive science is precisely what Discover surfaces. Every pillar article gets a 1200×630 lead image (the OG generator already makes one), a question or myth-busting H1, and a visible publish/updated date.

The E-E-A-T floor (one-time build, then automatic)

  • Real, consistent author identities — bio, photo, credentials, a linked profile. Pick two or three and stick to them.
  • A public Editorial standards page: how facts are checked, sources cited, what "reviewed" means.
  • Every article: 2–4 institutional sources, a "last reviewed" date, an author byline that links to the bio.
  • Organisation schema with the new logo (done), sameAs links to the real social accounts once they exist.

04 — YouTube

A Shorts channel that occasionally makes a long video.

The retention data is unambiguous: Shorts work, long-form doesn't. Stop fighting it.

Shorts — the growth engine

  • One per day, same time, forever. Consistency beats volume.
  • Hook is the first frame — no logo, no "in this video," straight to the surprising claim.
  • Burned-in captions the whole way (most viewing is muted).
  • 21–40 seconds. End on a line that makes the loop rewatch.
  • Title = the exact question someone would type. Description = one keyword-rich sentence + source link.

Long-form — paused, not cancelled

Produce a long video only when a Short crosses ~10k views. Then make the 6–8 minute version of that proven topic, link it from the Short's pinned comment and description, and add an end screen to the next one. Demand-driven, not calendar-driven.

Thumbnails & titles

The new template is live. Add the built-in A/B: generate two variants per long-form (question vs. statement, or two different `--visual` icons) and let YouTube's Test & Compare pick the winner on click-through. Drop knowthiswell.com from the thumbnail — invisible at grid size, and redundant with the handle.

Free surface area

Community tab 2–3×/week: a poll ("which of these is true?"), a single fact card, a "coming this week." Zero production cost, real reach, and it trains the algorithm that the channel is active between uploads.

05 — TikTok & Reels

The channel that doesn't exist yet — and has the most room.

TikTok gives brand-new accounts real distribution on day one. The format that already works for you as a Short is the native TikTok format. This is the single highest-upside move in the plan.

Build the arm into the pipeline

The same vertical render feeds three platforms with small per-platform edits:

PlatformWhat changes from the YouTube Short
TikTokStrip any "subscribe" CTA. Export watermark-free. Post natively — never as a link to YouTube. Caption carries the keyword phrase + 3 specific hashtags.
Instagram ReelsPick a clean cover frame. 3–5 hashtags. Keep it under 60s.
YouTube ShortsAs now — the "subscribe" ask is fine here.

TikTok's ranking signals (all automatable)

  • The keyword phrase appears three ways: spoken in the audio, shown as on-screen text, and written in the caption. TikTok transcribes and reads all three.
  • First second is the hook — the claim, on screen, immediately.
  • 3–4 specific hashtags (#physicsexplained, not #science). Broad tags don't help anymore.
  • Post 1–2×/day at a fixed time. Volume + consistency is how the FYP learns you.

The one semi-manual lever

Replying to a comment with a video is the strongest free-reach move on TikTok. If even 10 minutes a week ever opens up, that's where it goes — pick the best question from yesterday's comments, answer it as tomorrow's Short. Otherwise the pipeline runs without it.

06 — Reach ceiling

What automation can't buy — and the honest limit.

The near-zero-manual constraint has a real cost, and it's worth naming so the 90-day verdict is judged fairly.

LeverAutomatable?Notes
Short-form volume & cross-postingYesThe core engine. Fully scripted.
Google Discover trafficYesFreshness + images + curiosity headlines. No outreach needed.
Reddit / Quora / X presenceYes (capped)Genuine answers only, safe daily limits. Already running.
Backlinks / domain authorityNoNeeds outreach, PR, partnerships. This will lag. Accept it.
Comment replies / communityPartlyCommunity-tab posts yes; per-comment video replies need a human.
Press, guest posts, "Show HN"NoOne-time human pushes. Optional, high-leverage if hours appear.

The bet on the ceiling: short-form + Discover can plausibly reach a few thousand visits and views a day within 90 days on consistency alone. Getting past that — into the tens of thousands, into rankings for competitive terms — takes authority work no cron can do. The plan gets you to the first ceiling honestly; the second ceiling is a later conversation about trading some automation for a few hours a week.
